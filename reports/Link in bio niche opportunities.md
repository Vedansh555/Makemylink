# Win Indian fashion creators before going global

The best niche to attack is **fashion, beauty and lifestyle micro-creators in India**. Give them a beautiful, Liinks-style page that takes **UPI payments with no platform fee**, has a "shop my looks" grid for affiliate links, includes a live media kit for brand deals, and is **priced in rupees**. Grow it with Liinks' tactic of building each creator's page first and then sending it to them in a DM. This is the one segment where all three of the founder's ideas fit together: a design edge, a price edge, and an international audience. It is also the segment where the global leaders are weakest. Linktree's commerce features work only in the US, US tools bill in dollars, and none of them offers UPI. The Indian rivals are small, seed-stage companies that focus on payments and courses, not on design. The honest catch is money per user. Most Indian micro-creators earn under about $200 a month, so prices must stay around ₹99–499 a month. That makes this a strong beachhead you can win, but a slow path to large revenue unless you also sell the same product in US dollars to creators abroad. Two more facts matter for the founder's plan. First, the founder's main belief is only half true: about half of link-in-bio users are outside the US, but Liinks' own paying customers look mostly American. Second, Liinks' "10,000+ users, 50% paid" claim is not a stated fact. It comes from putting two different numbers side by side. A realistic result for a solo founder in this market is **$5–25K MRR after 2–4 years**, and that is what Liinks itself reached.

## Liinks is a one-person US business, and the "50% paid" number is made up from two figures

Liinks is a small, bootstrapped company. Its founder is Charlie Clark, a designer and engineer in Brooklyn, New York. He works alone, has no investors, and built the first version in about two weeks by reusing code from an earlier app ([Starter Story](https://starterstory.com/stories/liinks-supercharge-your-link-in-bio)). The business grew slowly but steadily. Its first customers paid **$3 a month**. It reached about $1,000 MRR a few months later and **$20,000 MRR by July 2023** ([Indie Hustle](https://indiehustle.beehiiv.com/p/simple-linkinbio-tool-making-20000-mrr)), with 4,200 subscribers at that point ([Superframeworks](https://superframeworks.com/blog/liinks)). By October 2024 it had **about $25K MRR and about 5,300 paying customers** (about 4,800 on the cheap plan and 500 on Pro), 80–90% profit margins, and only **11% growth over the past year** ([Starter Story](https://starterstory.com/stories/liinks-supercharge-your-link-in-bio)). One revenue tracker now shows about $31.5–32K MRR ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/liinks)). We could not confirm whether it reached its goal of $30K MRR by September 2025.

The "10,000+ users, 50% paid" claim does not hold up as a real number. The homepage says "Loved by 10,000+ creators & businesses" and gives no date ([liinks.co](https://liinks.co)). The 5,300 paying customers is a separate number from October 2024. Dividing one by the other gives "about 50%", but no source says this. The ratio also has little meaning, because **Liinks has no free plan**. Users can design a page for free, but to publish it they must start a 14-day trial and then pay $5 a month ($48 a year) for Starter or $12 a month for Pro, which adds custom domains and multiple profiles ([liinks.co](https://liinks.co)). Clark removed the free plan because of spam and phishing, and he says this *increased* the number of paying users ([Starter Story](https://starterstory.com/stories/liinks-supercharge-your-link-in-bio)). So the number to copy is not "50% paid". The lesson is that **making users pay to publish works better than a free plan in this market.**

Liinks' customers are not famous influencers. Its testimonials and reviews come from small businesses and service providers who also post as creators: real estate agents, estheticians, bridal hair and makeup artists, musicians, travel bloggers, speakers and agencies. Most of the reviewers whose country is visible are in the **US**, with a few in Australia, Canada, Germany and Greece ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/liinks.co); [liinks.co](https://liinks.co)). Most users arrive from Instagram ([Superframeworks](https://superframeworks.com/blog/liinks)). This matters for the founder's plan. Liinks did not win by serving foreign creators. It won by serving English-speaking Instagram small businesses with nicer design at a lower price.

Liinks grew in five ways. The first is the tactic the founder wants to copy. **Clark contacted people using rival tools and offered to rebuild their pages in Liinks, and he built pages for people who had no page at all and invited them to try it** ([Starter Story](https://starterstory.com/stories/liinks-supercharge-your-link-in-bio)). The second was a #8 finish on Product Hunt with about 300 upvotes, although some commenters called it "yet another link-in-bio tool". The third was paid Instagram ads, and later Google Ads after Apple's iOS 14 changes made Instagram ads more expensive. The fourth was a "Made with Liinks" badge on every page. The fifth was SEO comparison pages such as liinks.co/compare/later ([Liinks compare page](https://www.liinks.co/compare/later)). One feature stood out: links in a creator's Instagram captions are added to their Liinks page automatically ([Superframeworks](https://superframeworks.com/blog/liinks)). Note that Starter Story gives **no conversion rates for the outreach** and does not say whether it was done by DM or email. Also, growth slowed once ad spending fell, which suggests paid ads carried a large part of the growth.

The bigger, venture-funded design-first rivals took a different road. Hoo.be and Komi won celebrities through personal connections and earn money from brands and sales fees. Hoo.be users included Tom Brady and Jeff Bezos ([Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2022/10/21/business/how-boston-startup-became-social-media-choice-celebrities-like-brady-bezos)). Komi raised about $17M ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2570230)). Milkshake, a free mobile app aimed mainly at women building personal brands, reached 24,000 downloads in 95 countries during early access ([Campaign Brief](https://campaignbrief.com/envato-founders-launch-free-milkshake-app-for-instagrammers-to-make-instant-websites/)). Snipfeed was reportedly absorbed by Planoly ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/snipfeed)). The well-funded players have moved toward commerce, brand deals and banking. That leaves the "beautiful page for small creators" space to cheap indie tools, and that is where a new founder can compete.

## Price alone cannot win, because the floor is already near zero

The founder plans to compete on price, but the market is already very cheap. **Lnk.bio sells a plan for $0.99 a month or $9.99 once**, **Carrd costs $9–19 a year**, Bio.link's Pro plan costs about $2–7, Taplink costs $3–8, and Liinks costs $5 ([InBio](https://in.bio/blog/link-in-bio-fees); [UniLink](https://www.unilink.us/blog/what-is-bio-link-tool)). Linktree also already **sets prices by region and shows them in local currency** ([Linktree pricing](https://linktr.ee/s/pricing)). A lower dollar price is easy to copy and does not stand out. Lower *local* prices combined with *local payments* and better design is much harder to copy.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Linktree | Yes | $8 / $15 / $35 (raised Nov 2025 from $5 / $9 / $24) | 12% free, 9% Starter/Pro, 0% Premium (one source says this changed in Oct 2026) | Commerce features US-only; regional pricing |
| Beacons | Yes | $10 / $30 / $90–100 | 9% on free and $10 plans, 0% from $30 | Uses creator's Stripe or PayPal |
| Stan Store | No (14-day trial) | $29 / $99 | 0% (Stripe fees still apply) | Coaches and digital products |
| Hoo.be | Yes | $9.99 / $29.99 | 9% / 5% / 0% | Custom domain only on top plan |
| Milkshake | Yes | $2.99 / $6.99 / ~$8 | 12% / 9% / 7% | Mobile app only |
| Liinks | No (14-day trial) | $5 / $12 | Not published | Custom domain on Pro |
| Lnk.bio | Yes | $0.99 or $9.99 one-time | 5% on shop sales | Very cheap |
| Carrd | 3 sites | $9–19 per year | 0% (you embed Stripe/PayPal) | One-page site builder |

Sources for the table: [Replug](https://replug.io/blog/linktree-pricing), [InBio](https://in.bio/blog/link-in-bio-fees), [Beacons Help](https://help.beacons.ai/en/articles/4695681), [Revenuegeeks](https://revenuegeeks.com/guides/stan-store-pricing), [Hoo.be pricing](https://hoo.be/pricing), [Liinks help](https://help.liinks.co/help/article/what-plans-are-available), [OwnStreet](https://ownstreet.in/blog/best-linktree-alternatives-india-2026), [Linqin](https://linqin.in/answers/best-linktree-alternative-india). Many of these figures come from rival vendors' blogs, and some conflict. Check them at checkout before using them in marketing.

The table shows a clear pricing gap. Almost every storefront reaches **0% sales fees only at $30–35 a month** (Linktree Premium, Beacons, Hoo.be) or charges a flat $29 or more (Stan). A creator who sells $200–500 a month therefore pays either a 9–12% cut or a subscription that eats a large share of their income. Reviews show Beacons sellers reach the break-even point between plans at about **$220–333 a month in sales** ([Roborhythms](https://www.roborhythms.com/beacons-ai-review/)). **No tool we checked offers 0% fees, local payment methods and a free custom domain at a low price.**

Creator complaints focus on money and trust more than on missing features. Linktree has 7,163 Trustpilot reviews with a 4.1 score, and the negative ones complain about surprise charges, price increases for long-time customers, and support that is only a chatbot. One reviewer in October 2026 wrote that "the price has more than doubled" ([Trustpilot Linktree](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/linktr.ee)). Stan reviewers complain about slow payouts, stores locked "due to traffic", and being charged after they cancel ([Trustpilot Stan](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/stan.store)). Beacons scores 2.0, with complaints about affiliate payouts stuck for over a year and an artist who could not get back their domain after cancelling ([Trustpilot Beacons](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/beacons.ai)). Creators also worry that Instagram will flag shared link domains. Instagram once blocked Linktree links as spam and later apologized ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linktree)). Finally, an entire group of India-only tools exists because Linktree **bills in USD and offers no UPI** ([OwnStreet](https://ownstreet.in/blog/best-linktree-alternatives-india-2026); [Linqin](https://linqin.in/answers/linqin-vs-linktree)). We could not open Reddit, G2 or Capterra, so this list of complaints comes from Trustpilot and vendor blogs, not from direct creator surveys.

## India fashion creators rank first among eight possible niches

About **49% of link-in-bio users are in the US**, according to a November 2024 analysis of 100 million Instagram profiles. The same study found that 64% of users are women, 77% have under 5,000 followers, about 46% are businesses, and #fashion is one of the top four hashtags ([influencers.club](https://influencers.club/blog/state-of-the-link-in-bio-market/)). So roughly half of users are outside the US, which partly supports the founder's belief. But money per user is lower outside the US, and Linktree's own **commerce features (Shops, Sponsored Links, Rewards) are limited to US users** ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/chat/h/aab1e515-45a7-413c-82c7-dbf6ff815f61/)). Earning money from non-US creators is a space the leader has left open.

The table below ranks the niches by how realistic it is to become #1 *and* make real revenue. Audience sizes for the lower rows are not well sourced, and the price levels are partly our estimates.

| Rank | Niche | Why it is open | Willingness to pay | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **India fashion, beauty and lifestyle micro-creators** | 2–2.5M monetized creators; no UPI from global tools; local rivals are small and focused on payments, not design | Low (₹99–499 a month) | Low revenue per user; many small local rivals |
| 2 | Brazil "link na bio" (Pix + Hotmart/Kiwify links) | Large digital-product economy; main rival is Taplink | Low to medium (about R$18–50) | Pix support of rivals not checked; needs Portuguese |
| 3 | US/UK fashion affiliate hub (LTK + ShopMy + Amazon in one page, plus media kit) | LTK and ShopMy pages are branded and separate | Medium to high ($10–30) | Very crowded (Linktree, Beacons, Liinks) |
| 4 | Adult creators (safe-for-work hub pages, age gate) | Linktree has restricted them; current tools are low quality | High | Payment processors, reputation, abuse |
| 5 | Gulf Arabic creators (right-to-left, bilingual, licence number display) | No Arabic-first tool found | High | Small market; not validated |
| 6 | Real estate agents | Need listings, booking, lead capture | High | Brokerage sites; buyer count not sourced |
| 7 | DJs, tattoo artists, models (booking, deposits, portfolio) | Underserved, but no data | Medium | Unknown size |
| 8 | Coaches and fitness | — | Medium ($29 norm) | Already owned by Stan and Whop |

India leads because of its size and because outreach is easy. BCG counts **2–2.5 million monetized creators in India, but only 8–10% monetize effectively**, compared with 40% or more in mature markets. Most nano and micro creators earn **under ₹18,000 a month (about $200)**, and more than 60% of consumers in smaller cities are influenced by creators ([BCG, May 2025](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/india-from-content-to-commerce-mapping-indias-creator-economy)). The local rivals are real but thinly funded. Cosmofeed's SuperProfile is a direct Linktree rival for Indian micro-creators, but it raised only a $1.5M seed in 2022 ([BusinessWorld](https://businessworld.in/article/superprofile-by-cosmofeed-a-tool-for-content-creators-to-effectively-monetise-their-content-464911); [Entrepreneur India](https://india.entrepreneur.com/finance/cosmofeed-raises-15-million-in-seed-round/422369)). Topmate focuses on paid consultations ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/company/topmate-io/)). OwnStreet, Linqin, Lynkova and Playto sell mainly on UPI, Razorpay and rupee billing ([Lynkova](https://lynkova.in/link-in-bio-platform-india); [Playto](https://www.playto.so/blogs/best-link-in-bio-indian-creators)). Our judgment is that **none of them is clearly the best-looking page for India's fashion and beauty creators**, and that is the gap a design-first founder can fill. No survey proves this gap. It comes from how these products describe themselves.

Brazil comes a close second and could rank first for a Portuguese-speaking founder. Its creators already sell digital products at scale. Kiwify has **30,000+ active creators and 25M+ buyers** ([Finsiders](https://finsidersbrasil.com.br/conteudo-de-marca/kiwify-conclui-nova-rodada-de-captacao-de-r-150-milhoes-com-seu-fidc)), Hotmart reports **US$10B in total partner sales** ([Mercado&Consumo](https://mercadoeconsumo.com.br/21/03/2024/inovacao/hotmart-plataforma-de-venda-online-alcanca-us-10-bilhoes-em-vendas-de-parceiros/)), and 42% of surveyed creators say digital product sales are their main income ([FGV](https://portal.fgv.br/en/noticia/digital-content-creation-grows-30-and-boosts-brazilian-job-market-survey-shows)). Taplink already targets Brazil with Portuguese "link na bio" content ([Taplink PT-BR](https://taplink.at/pt-br/blog/como-criar-um-linktree.html)). Brazilian creators likely pay more than Indian ones, but language and the competition are bigger barriers for an outsider.

The US/UK fashion affiliate hub has the strongest buyers, but it is the hardest fight. LTK creators drive **more than $6B a year in retail sales** ([BusinessWire](https://secure.businesswire.com/news/home/20260901107046/en/LTK-Names-the-Creators-and-Brands-Shaping-Culture-and-Commerce-in-the-2026-LTK-Awards)), and ShopMy has about 175,000 creators ([Stack Influence](https://stackinfluence.com/blog/shopmy-vs-ltk-best-affiliate-platform-2026)). But this is exactly where Linktree, Beacons and Liinks already compete. The good news is that the product built for Indian fashion creators *is* this product. Selling it in dollars to fashion creators abroad is a natural second step, not a new build.

Adult creators have the highest willingness to pay and the weakest tools. Linktree removed a wave of adult-creator accounts in January 2022 ([WhaleFinders](https://whalefinders.com/blog/onlyfans-link-in-bio-optimization-2026)), and OnlyFans had **$4.8B in sales volume in 2022** ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/linktree-link-in-bio-onlyfans-dmz/)). But payment processors, Instagram bans and abuse make it a poor first market for a small team. Liinks removed its free plan precisely because of abuse.

## The whole market is small, and a solo founder should expect $5–25K MRR

The creator economy is huge, about **$250B in 2023 and heading to $480B by 2027** ([Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/pages/the-creator-economy-could-approach-half-a-trillion-dollars-by-2027.html)). Most of that money is brand deals and ad payouts, though, not spending on tools. Market reports put link-in-bio tools at anywhere from **$0.4B to $1.8B** for 2025 ([Market Intelo](https://marketintelo.com/report/link-in-bio-tools-market); [linkie.bio](https://linkie.bio/blog/link-in-bio-tools)). These reports are low quality and disagree with each other. Adding up the real companies gives a smaller number, about $0.2–0.5B a year in subscriptions. That figure is our estimate.

The leader earns very little per user. Linktree has about **70M users** ([SmartCompany](https://www.smartcompany.com.au/startupsmart/linktree-acquires-leibovich-brothers-backed-fingertip-to-shut-it-down/)) but only an estimated **$37M (2023) to $55.5M (2024) in revenue** ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/linktree/)). That is under $1 per user per year, which suggests only about 1% of users pay (our estimate; Linktree does not disclose this). Beacons converted only **about 0.5% of free users to paid** ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/beacons)), while typical software companies convert 2–8% ([Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/freemium-to-premium)). Stan shows the opposite model. It has no free plan, charges $29 a month, and reached **$28.3M ARR by the end of 2024** with about 80,000 active creators. But it loses **about 13% of customers every month**, and its growth slowed to about 24% in 2025 ([Contrary](https://research.contrary.com/company/stan); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/stans-shopify-vs-whops-amazon/)). The lesson: **free plans barely convert in this category, so make users pay to publish. Then fight churn by being useful every week, through payments, brand deals and analytics.**

For independent founders, the realistic ceiling is clear. Liinks reached about $25K MRR in 3–4 years with one person ([Starter Story](https://starterstory.com/stories/liinks-supercharge-your-link-in-bio)). Carrd went further, past $100K MRR with two people, but only because it became a broader one-page website builder ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/growing-carrd-to-2m-arr-in-a-crowded-market-with-zero-marketing-by-limiting-features-dbeoP4bDlEL3zzsivdgj)). Linktree also buys smaller rivals and shuts them down. It has done this with Bento, Koji, Plann and Fingertip ([SmartCompany](https://www.smartcompany.com.au/startupsmart/linktree-acquires-leibovich-brothers-backed-fingertip-to-shut-it-down/)), so a sale to Linktree is one possible exit. A simple calculation for India shows the challenge. At an average of about ₹250 a month (about $2.80), **$10K MRR needs about 3,500 paying creators**. Liinks has about 5,300 payers after four years. That number is reachable, but it takes years, which is why we recommend adding a dollar-priced plan for creators abroad from the start.

## The recommended plan: a rupee-priced, design-first page for Indian fashion creators

**Positioning.** "The most beautiful link page for Indian fashion and beauty creators. Get paid by UPI. Pay 0% fees. Pay in rupees." The page should look like a creator's own brand, not a list of buttons. Lead with three proven pains: Linktree's dollar billing and missing UPI, the 9–12% fee on cheap plans, and pages that all look the same.

**Pricing.** Copy Liinks' model: building is free, but publishing requires a 14-day trial and then payment. This filters out spam and avoids the 0.5–1% conversion trap of free plans. Our suggested prices are a Starter plan of about **₹149 a month (₹1,199 a year)** and a Pro plan of about **₹399 a month**. Pro adds a custom domain, selling, and the media kit, and charges **0% platform fee** (only the payment gateway's fee). This puts Starter at the low end of the local range (₹99–499) and Pro just above OwnStreet's ₹299 ([OwnStreet](https://ownstreet.in/blog/best-linktree-alternatives-india-2026)). For creators outside India, offer the same product at **about $4 and $9 a month**, just under Liinks ($5/$12) and Linktree ($8/$15). Do not chase the $0.99 floor. Buyers at that price do not stay, and you cannot fund support with them.

**Must-have MVP features.** The aim is to beat rivals on what creators complain about, not to match every feature.

| Feature | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| 15–20 premium templates made for fashion and beauty (photo grids, "look" cards, brand colours) | Design is the main edge; it is how Liinks won |
| One-click import from Instagram and from Linktree pages | Lets you build a creator's page in minutes for outreach |
| UPI checkout through Razorpay or a similar gateway, with 0% platform fee on Pro | The main gap left by Linktree and Beacons in India |
| "Shop my looks" grid for affiliate links and discount codes | Fashion creators juggle many affiliate links |
| Live media kit (followers, engagement, rate card) | Helps creators win brand deals, which make up about 70% of creator income ([Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/pages/the-creator-economy-could-approach-half-a-trillion-dollars-by-2027.html)) |
| WhatsApp button and enquiry form | WhatsApp is the normal business channel in India |
| Hindi and regional-language text support | Many creators post in local languages |
| Basic analytics, QR code, "Made with" badge | The badge was Liinks' viral loop |
| Custom domain on Pro, and domain stays with the creator if they leave | Answers fears about shared-domain flagging and domain lock-in |
| Fast human support (WhatsApp or email, reply within 24 hours) | Support is a top complaint about every big tool; Liinks replies to all negative reviews ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/liinks.co)) |

**Go-to-market playbook.** The "build their page first, then DM" tactic is proven to have worked for Liinks, but no conversion data exists. Run it as a measured experiment.

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| 1. Make a target list | Each day, find 30–50 Indian fashion, beauty or lifestyle creators with roughly 10k–200k followers. Start with those whose bio shows a Linktree link with Linktree branding, or who have no link at all. These creators are easy to find, and their old page gives you a ready "before" picture. |
| 2. Build the page first | Use your import tool to make each creator a page in their own colours and photos in under 10 minutes. Keep it **private, as an unlisted preview link**, and never publish someone's page without their permission. |
| 3. Send the DM | Write a short, personal message in English or Hindi: one compliment about their content, a screenshot of the new page, the private preview link, and the offer "It's yours free for 2 months if you want it. I'll move all your links for you." |
| 4. Follow up once | After 3 days, send one polite follow-up and nothing more. Delete unclaimed previews after 14 days. |
| 5. Measure | Track replies, claims, and paid conversions after the trial. As a working goal (not a benchmark), aim for a few percent of DMs to turn into paying users. If results are much lower, change the message or the target group before sending more. |
| 6. Turn users into marketers | Add the "Made with" badge, a referral reward (one free month for each paying friend), and ask happy creators to post a story. Stan grew mainly through creator referral loops ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/chat/h/7ca39735-0ffb-42a2-919b-80bca1932b5a)). |
| 7. Add SEO and agencies | Publish comparison pages ("Linktree alternative India", "Linktree vs [your brand]") as Liinks does. Pitch influencer agencies, which manage many creator pages, on a multi-profile plan. |
| 8. Expand | Once India works, sell the same templates in dollars to fashion creators in the UK, the Gulf (with Arabic and right-to-left support) and the Indian diaspora. |

**Risks.** The first risk is Instagram itself. Sending many similar DMs from a new account can get it limited, so send fewer, more personal messages from a real, warmed-up account, and add email or WhatsApp where creators list them. Instagram's own multi-link bio is another quiet competitor ([Coffee & Contracts](https://coffeecontracts.com/blog/10-instagram-features-you-may-not-know-about-for-real-estate-agents)). The second risk is low revenue per user. Rupee prices make $10K MRR a multi-year goal, so the dollar plan matters. The third risk is that Linktree or a funded Indian rival adds UPI and better templates. Your defence is speed, design quality, close relationships with creators, and building on Instagram-specific features as Liinks did. The fourth risk is payments. Payment gateways require KYC checks, and creators will leave quickly if payouts are slow, as Stan's and Beacons' reviews show. The fifth risk is abuse. Free pages attract spam and phishing, which is why Liinks removed its free plan. The sixth risk is churn. Stan loses about 13% of customers a month, so give creators reasons to come back weekly, such as sales alerts, media-kit updates and brand-deal leads.

## Conclusion

The founder's two instincts need small corrections. "Most customers are foreign" is true for *users*, about half of whom are outside the US, but not for *paying* customers at Liinks. And "compete on price" only works when price means **local currency, local payments and no sales fee**, not a lower dollar number, because the dollar floor is already below $1. The winning idea is not a cheaper Linktree. It is a product that fits a local market so well that Linktree's US-built commerce cannot follow: UPI, rupees, WhatsApp, and design made for Indian fashion. That is a niche one founder can realistically lead.

The most useful thing to learn early is something no source could provide: **how many "build first, then DM" previews turn into paying users**. If the first 500 DMs bring in even a few dozen paying creators, the India plan works, and the same templates can then be sold in dollars abroad, which is where most of the revenue will come from. If they do not, Brazil (Pix plus Hotmart links) is the best second market to test with the same playbook.
